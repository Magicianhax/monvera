Vera builds a plan by reasoning over about twelve months of real price history plus live prices, then attaching a plain risk read to each holding. She is not clairvoyant. She can be wrong, and nothing about signing a plan changes that.

## What Vera reasons over

Vera works from two inputs, both real and both checkable. The first is about twelve months of real equity price history for each asset, the same history the [screener](/use/use-the-screener/) and [backtests](/how/open-strategies-and-backtests/) draw from. The second is live prices at plan time: Chainlink feeds where a feed is published, and firm Arcus RFQ quotes for the executable price. Where those prices come from, and which assets have no Chainlink feed, is documented on [where prices come from](/how/where-prices-come-from/).

From that, Vera produces a named plan (for example "Steady Growth" over Apple, Nvidia, the S&P 500, and US Treasuries), a one-line reason per holding, and an assessed risk. The assessed risk is what she signs into the `RiskInference` struct, bounded by a risk ceiling, when the plan is recorded on-chain.

## Where Vera can be wrong

Vera's inputs are backward-looking, and past prices do not predict future ones. Twelve months of history captures how an asset moved; it does not tell you how it will move. A plan Vera assessed as steady can still fall, because these are real companies and real prices, and prices fall. You can lose money; see [can I lose money](/safety/can-i-lose-money/).

Vera outputs options with reasons and a risk read, not directives. A plan is a basket you judge before you commit, not a recommendation to act on. Nothing Vera returns is investment advice.

## What a signature does and does not prove

<Aside type="caution">
Vera signs the risk assessment; you sign the spend. Her signature proves she authored that exact assessment at that time. It does not prove the assessment is correct, and it does not promise a return.
</Aside>

The on-chain record makes Vera's track record honest, not infallible. Because her signed assessment is recorded in the same transaction as the trades (see [how the plan is signed and recorded](/how/accountable-ai/)), her calls cannot be quietly deleted or rewritten after the fact. That accountability means a wrong call stays on the record too. The value of the signature is that you can hold the assessment to what actually happened, not that the assessment was right.

For the honest read on backtests as history rather than promises, see [backtests are history, not promises](/safety/backtests-are-history-not-promises/).
