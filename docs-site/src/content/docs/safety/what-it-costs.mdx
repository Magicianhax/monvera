Monvera charges you nothing. The only cost in the whole flow is the trading venue's spread, and it is already inside the quote you see before you confirm.

| What | You pay |
| --- | --- |
| Platform fee | $0 |
| Account fee or subscription | $0 |
| Network (gas) fee | $0 — Monvera sponsors it |
| Trading venue spread | The one real cost, already in your quote |
| [Groves](/use/groves/) (when live) | $0 to enter, hold, or rebalance — 10% of realized profit, only when you exit |

## The $1 minimum, and the $11 that actually matters

You can start with $1. But each leg of a plan needs about $11, because that is the trading venue's minimum order size.

Both numbers are true and they pull against each other. $30 buys one name comfortably. $30 split across six names is six legs of $5, and none of them can fill. Starting small means fewer names, not thinner slices:

  <Turn who="you">I've only got $30 — can I do the AI basket?</Turn>
  <Turn who="vera">Not across six names. That's $5 a leg and the venue won't fill under about $11. Two names at $15 each works — want that?</Turn>

## What the spread is

The gap between the price to buy and the price to sell at that moment. The market sets it, not Monvera, and it is wider on thinly traded names than on the S&P 500. It applies to every trade whatever the size, so it is not a fee you can grow out of.

## Why gas is free

Every trade on Robinhood Chain carries a network cost. Monvera pays it for you, so you never fund a separate balance, never hold a gas token, and never see a line item for it.

## How Monvera makes money

The trading venue pays Monvera a referral fee on the volume routed to it. That comes out of the venue's side rather than being added to your trade. It is the entire business model, and it is the reason there is no platform fee to charge you instead.
