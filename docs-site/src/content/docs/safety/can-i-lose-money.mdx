Yes. A $100 basket can be worth $80 next week. Every holding in a Monvera plan is a real company or fund, and the price of a real company goes down as well as up.

## Why the value moves

The value moves because you own real companies, not a fixed savings balance. When you invest the "Steady Growth" plan over Apple (AAPL), Nvidia (NVDA), the S&P 500 (SPY), and US Treasuries (SGOV), you own a share of each of those at its live market price. If Apple falls 10% tomorrow, your Apple holding falls with it. You can see the same live prices anyone else sees on the [explore page](/use/explore-whats-available/).

A mix of several companies and funds tends to swing less than a single stock, which is why Vera builds a basket rather than a bet. It does not remove loss. A basket can still be worth less than you put in.

## What Monvera is not

Monvera is not a bank and your money is not insured. There is no deposit protection, no guaranteed return, and nobody who covers your losses. If a plan drops in value, that loss is real and it is yours.

<Aside type="caution" title="Only invest money you can afford to lose">
Prices can fall the day after you invest and stay down for a long time. Put in an amount that would not hurt your rent or your bills if it dropped by a third.
</Aside>

## What is actually true here

You keep control of your money the whole time, and you can sell back to dollars at any point. Your money sits in [your own account](/safety/your-account-and-recovery/), Monvera cannot move it without your approval, and you can [take it out](/use/withdraw-your-money/) whenever you want. That is a real protection. It protects you from Monvera, not from the market.

Two more honest reads worth knowing before you invest:

- Vera's plans are options with reasons and a risk read, not [investment advice](/safety/is-this-investment-advice/).
- Any past performance you see is a [backtest, which is history and not a promise](/safety/backtests-are-history-not-promises/).
