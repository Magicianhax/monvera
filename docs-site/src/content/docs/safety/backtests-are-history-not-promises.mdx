A backtest shows what a rule would have done on past prices. It is history, not a promise. The raw numbers behind every strategy are open for anyone to read at [https://monvera.best/api/strategies](https://monvera.best/api/strategies), so you can check the figures instead of taking a chart on faith.

## What a backtest is

A backtest runs a fixed rule over real price history and reports what that rule returned in the past. When a strategy shows a past return, that number came from running its rule across real prices of companies like Apple (AAPL) and the S&P 500 (SPY) over a past window. It is a measurement of history, and it is real. It is a record of what already happened.

## What a backtest is not

A backtest is not a forecast of what you will earn. The future does not repeat the past on schedule. A rule that returned well over the last few years can return badly over the next few, and a backtest cannot tell you which. It does not include every cost, every gap in trading, or every event that has not happened yet. Treat the number as a fact about the past and nothing more.

<Aside type="caution" title="A good backtest is not a good outcome">
Judge a strategy by its rule and its assumptions, not only by its past return. A high past number can come from a narrow window that will not repeat. The plan you invest can still lose money.
</Aside>

## Check the numbers yourself

The raw JSON is public, so you do not have to trust a summary. Open [https://monvera.best/api/strategies](https://monvera.best/api/strategies) to read each strategy's rule, its assumptions, and its walk-forward backtest as plain data. For how these strategies are built and what the assumptions mean, see [open strategies and backtests](/how/open-strategies-and-backtests/).
