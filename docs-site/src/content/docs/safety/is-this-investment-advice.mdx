No. Vera's baskets are options with reasons and a plain risk read, not recommendations. Nothing on Monvera is investment advice, and nobody at Monvera is telling you what you should do with your money.

## What Vera actually gives you

Vera gives you a named basket you can accept, change, or ignore. When you ask for something like steady growth over five years, she returns the "Steady Growth" plan over Apple (AAPL), Nvidia (NVDA), the S&P 500 (SPY), and US Treasuries (SGOV), each holding with a one-line reason it is there and a plain read on what could go wrong. That is a starting point for your own judgment, not a directive.

You decide. You can [change any amount or drop any company](/use/tweak-a-plan-before-you-invest/) before you invest, and nothing happens until you approve the spend yourself.

## Why the reasons are not a promise

The reasons explain Vera's thinking, and thinking can be wrong. Vera reasons over past price history and live prices, and she is not able to see the future. A reason tells you why a company is in the basket. It does not promise the company will go up. For how she decides and where she can be wrong, see [how Vera decides](/how/how-vera-decides/).

## What her signature does and does not mean

Vera signs the risk assessment behind each plan with her own on-chain key, and that record proves she authored that assessment. It does not make the plan advice, and it does not promise a return. You separately approve the spend, which is the only step that moves your money. The two are different: Vera signs the risk assessment, you sign the spend. See [accountable AI](/how/accountable-ai/) for what that signature proves and what it does not.

You can still lose money on any plan. See [can I lose money](/safety/can-i-lose-money/). For where Monvera is available and the full standing on availability and advice, see [where it works](/start/where-its-available/).
