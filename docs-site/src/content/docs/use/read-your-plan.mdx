You read a plan one row at a time, and each row tells you the same three things: which real company or fund it is, why Vera put it there, and one honest line on what could go wrong. Reading the plan is how you judge the basket before you commit a cent, so take it slowly.

## What each row shows

Each row is one real company or fund, with the parts you need to decide about it in one place:

- **The real company and its live price.** Apple shows as Apple with Apple's price and chart, not a code or a symbol you have to decode.
- **Its share of the plan.** How much of your amount goes into that one holding, so you can see where the weight sits.
- **Vera's one-line reason.** Why this company is in this basket, in plain words.
- **A plain risk read.** One honest line on what could go wrong with that holding.

## Read the Steady Growth example

The **Steady Growth** plan holds four things, and reading it shows what a good plan looks like. Each row pairs a reason with a risk read, so you are never sold on the upside alone:

- **Apple (AAPL).** A large, steady company. The plain read is that even big companies fall, so this is not a sure thing.
- **Nvidia (NVDA).** Faster growth and larger swings. The plain read is that it can drop hard in a bad month, more than the others here.
- **The S&P 500.** A fund holding 500 large US companies, so no single company sinks you. The plain read is that the whole market still falls in a downturn.
- **US Treasuries.** Short-term government debt, the calm part of the basket that barely moves. The plain read is that it grows slowly and is there to steady the rest.

The four together are more spread out than any one of them alone. That spread is the point of a basket: a bad month for Nvidia does not have to be a bad month for the whole plan.

## Judge it before you commit

Read the reason and the risk read on every row, and only keep the ones you understand. If a row's risk read worries you, drop it or lower its amount before you invest, covered in [Tweak a plan before you invest](/use/tweak-a-plan-before-you-invest/). Nothing is bought until you tap invest.

<Aside type="caution">
A reason and a risk read are not a promise. Every company in a plan can lose value, and a $100 basket can be worth $80 next week. Read [Can I lose money?](/safety/can-i-lose-money/) before you invest.
</Aside>

## Why the plan is one you can trust

Vera signs the risk assessment behind your plan with her own on-chain key, and that record is written down permanently when your trade happens, so her track record cannot be edited later. You separately approve the spend. These are two different actions: Vera signs the risk assessment, you sign the spend. The record proves she authored the assessment, not that the plan will make money. How that record works, and how you can check it yourself, is on [Accountable AI](/how/accountable-ai/).
