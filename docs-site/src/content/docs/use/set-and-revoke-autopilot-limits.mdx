You turn Autopilot on by setting its four limits and granting it permission to invest for you, and you can switch it off at any time. Switching it off removes that permission, so nothing more can run. The four limits are the amount per run, how often it runs, a risk ceiling, and a total spend cap, explained in [invest on a schedule with Autopilot](/use/autopilot/).

## Turn Autopilot on

<Steps>

1. Open the Autopilot screen and choose the amount to invest each run, for example $20.

2. Choose how often it runs, for example once a week.

3. Set your risk ceiling, the most risk you will accept on any run.

4. Set your total spend cap, the most Autopilot may ever invest across all runs.

5. Grant Autopilot permission to place those scheduled buys for you, and confirm. Autopilot is now on and will run on your schedule inside the four limits.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
Once Autopilot is on, it invests real money on its own schedule until you switch it off or the spend cap is reached. You can still lose money, because prices fall. Review [can I lose money?](/safety/can-i-lose-money/) before the first scheduled run.
</Aside>

## Change your limits

You can change any of the four limits at any time from the Autopilot screen. New limits apply to every run from that point on, and past runs are unchanged. Lowering the spend cap below what Autopilot has already invested stops future runs, since the running total is already at or above the cap.

## Switch Autopilot off

<Steps>

1. Open the Autopilot screen.

2. Tap to switch Autopilot off.

3. Confirm. This removes the permission you granted, so no further run can happen.

</Steps>

Switching Autopilot off stops all future runs at once. It does not sell anything you already own; your existing holdings stay yours, and you can [take your money out](/use/withdraw-your-money/) whenever you want. You can turn Autopilot back on later by setting the limits and granting permission again.
