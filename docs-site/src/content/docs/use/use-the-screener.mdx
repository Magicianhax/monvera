## What the four figures mean

Each column is a plain measure of how a company's price has behaved. Sort by any of them to bring the top or bottom of that measure to the front.

- **1-year return.** How much the price changed over the last twelve months, as a percentage. A company at plus 40 percent rose over the year; one at minus 15 percent fell.
- **3-month return.** The same idea over the last three months, so you can see recent moves separately from the full year.
- **Volatility.** How much the price bounces around. A higher number means bigger swings up and down, which is a rougher ride to hold.
- **Max drawdown.** The biggest drop from a past high to the low that followed, as a percentage. It tells you the worst fall a holder would have sat through.

A company with no price history for a figure is flagged as such rather than shown a made-up number, so an empty cell means the data is missing, not zero.

## Sort the list

<Steps>

1. Open the screener screen.

2. Tap the column you want to order by: 1-year return, 3-month return, volatility, or max drawdown.

3. Tap the same column again to flip between highest-first and lowest-first.

4. Tap any row to open that company, see its full chart, and [save it to your watchlist](/use/build-a-watchlist/) or [buy it](/use/buy-or-sell-a-single-stock/).

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
These are past figures, not predictions. A company that rose the most last year can fall this year, and a low-volatility company can still lose you money. The figures help you compare what already happened, nothing more. For why past numbers are not promises, see [backtests are history, not promises](/safety/backtests-are-history-not-promises/).
</Aside>

## Turn a shortlist into a plan

Once the screener has narrowed the list, you have two ways forward. Buy a single company on its own at its live price, or hand Vera a goal and let her [build a diversified plan](/use/make-your-first-plan/) that may include companies you found here. Nothing on the screener is a recommendation; it is a way to sort real figures.
